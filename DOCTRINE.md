# Doctrine

The Stewart Family App is the family's living-room screen. Think of the enchanted pages in Harry Potter: cream paper where the ink moves. It shows one beautiful thing at a time (an idea, a quote, a question, someday a photograph), with the hour, the sky, and what's next written quietly in the corners. Ask it, and it turns the page.

These are the laws. A change that breaks one is a bug, even if someone asked for it. To change a law, change this file first, out loud, in its own commit, with the reason.

The one-word test for every decision: **magic.** It should feel magic and effortless. If it needs explaining, a setting, or a second try, it isn't done.

---

## I. Purpose

1. **It serves the family. It never competes for them.** The app is part of the room, not a feed. Success is a glance, a smile, a question at dinner, and then everyone goes back to their lives. No feeds, no scrolling, no notifications, no badges, no streaks, no autoplay (except §3's nature). The one moving picture is the family's own nature clips (§3): a minute now and then, or, when someone asks for nature, one after another until someone asks for something else or an hour has passed. We never measure how long anyone looked. A feature whose main effect is that someone stays longer needs a better reason than that, or it doesn't ship.

2. **Less is more.** Home shows one thing at a time, each for about half a minute, in turn, and it always opens on the weather. Until noon, when the day is being planned, the weather comes first: two views of it, then a quote. From noon to midnight the weather matters less: one view of it, then two quotes, or a quote and the question for the table. About once a quarter hour, a cycle closes on a minute of nature instead (§3). Everything else is a small note in a corner or lives one page away. Before adding anything to a screen, try taking something away.

3. **Alive, never busy.** The page is a living picture, like a window. Its sky moves the way a real one does: clouds drift, stars breathe, rain falls, snow settles, and the light warms and cools with the hour. That life is ambient: slow, continuous, organic, never visibly looping, and it never asks to be watched. A rare surprise is allowed (a shooting star, birds crossing, a cloud lit from within), as long as it glides rather than flashes. Nothing blinks, flickers, ticks, bounces, or counts down. The piece itself changes when the hour turns, when home turns to its next piece (every half minute, by day and by night), or when someone asks. One exception: the weather's hours and days turn through their lenses (the sky, rain and snow, the air, how it feels, wind, humidity, UV), skipping any lens with nothing to say. Asked for, they turn every half minute, opening on the sky; as a slide on home or at night, each visit shows the next lens, so every lens comes round without anyone asking. Their labels hold still; only the figures re-ink, one after another, so the page is never blank. A long list of games turns its leaves the same way, half a minute each, line by line. Never to catch an eye. The other exception is nature: about once a quarter hour, a minute of a real place, full screen; asked for, clip after clip for up to an hour. Natural sound only, gentle and even; nothing frightening; at night it is dimmed. Over it, the page's notes stay, at the top, out of the picture's way.

4. **Dim at night.** Night is the real night where the family lives, and it falls the way light really goes. From sunset the day's paper dims with the sky through civil twilight, and the light lingers on the horizon where the sun went down, unless cloud or smoke hides it. Halfway through, as the first stars come out, the paper turns dark: the page sinks first, and nothing is ever written on the paper while it changes. Then the stars come out one by one, brightest first. Dawn runs the same way backward, and the paper is full day at sunrise. Through the night the page turns as it does by day, one thing at a time, each for half a minute, in the same mix (§2): the weather, and the night page, with the time, what's next (tonight's plan or tomorrow's first), one quiet piece, and the night sky. All of it in dim ink, with no accent. The twilight dims only the day's paper, never the night's ink. At night a nature clip still plays, dimmed to the night paper (its brightest white no brighter than the night ink) and softer, its notes in night ink. A bright screen in a dark room is a bug, and so is a word you can't read. A page asked for at night (the games, the weather) follows the same rule: the night's paper and ink, and no accent.

5. **Everything returns home.** Any page someone asks for goes back home by itself. No one ever has to clean up after the app.

6. **Effortless or not at all.** The family never configures, logs in twice, or waits on a spinner. One key pairs a device for a year. Voice, phone, and remote all lead to the same few pages. If a feature needs instructions on the fridge, it isn't finished.

## II. Craft

7. **Fewer things, finished.** One beautiful page beats four good ones. We would rather ship nothing than something half-made. A new page or feature has to earn its place against what's already here; until then it waits in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

8. **Enchanted paper. It's called an app; it never looks like one.** Ink on cream paper. Type does the work, with thin rules for structure. No emoji, app icons, cards, shadows, rounded corners, or gradients beyond the paper itself. Small symbols are drawn as fine ink engravings (`src/views/Engraving.tsx`), never borrowed icon sets. A nature clip is the one exception to the paper: it fills the screen, because a real place deserves the whole window, and the page's ink sits at its head as a few notes, never a player (no controls, progress, or titles). It is not a newspaper either: no mastheads, editions, headlines, or press jargon. It's the family's own enchanted page.

9. **One face on the TV.** On trial with the Almanac design (Nov. 16, 2026): Fraunces for every page Almanac draws (home, night, weather, the games). Week and Ideas keep IM Fell English and its small caps until they're redrawn in Fraunces; then Fell goes. UnifrakturMaguntia appears only on the lock screen and the phone. Colors come only from the paper tokens (`--paper`, `--ink`, `--ink-soft`, `--ink-faint`, `--rule`, `--accent`, in `globals.css` and the design's block in `src/views/designs/designs.css`). A clip's colors are its own, and over it the ink turns light by day. The accent (oxblood) appears at most once per page. By day the paper is light and milky, never brown; at night it is nearly black, with the ink no brighter than a soft gray.

10. **Ink, never pixels.** Content arrives and leaves by bleeding into the paper (`src/components/Ink.tsx`), in one of a few hands: _seep_ (a slow stain), _write_ (lines appear as if written), _develop_ (a photograph surfacing, for photos only), and the original _blots_. Nothing slides, pops, fades on a timer, or cuts. A nature clip is the one thing that fades, by opacity alone (the TV can't filter moving pictures): the sky sinks, then the clip surfaces; it sinks before anything else comes, and the notes come only while it's full. Every block of type on the TV sits inside an `<Ink>`. When the ink is dry, the filter comes off. The sky's own life (§3) is drawn, not inked, and costs the TV almost nothing: whole layers move and fade (transform and opacity), and nothing repaints frame by frame. While a clip plays, the sky behind it stops and hides: the TV's GPU has the clip alone.

11. **Readable from the couch, and generous.** The TV is a fixed 120 × 67.5 rem sheet (1 rem = 16 px at 1080p). Use the whole sheet: the piece and the sky are drawn big, edge to edge, and nothing that matters is small. Anything meant to be read from across the room is at least 2.5 rem; corner notes at least 1.85 rem. (Week and Ideas keep the old minimums, 1.85 and 1.3 rem, until they're redrawn.) Content stays inside the margins (5.5 rem sides, 3 rem top and bottom), which clears TV overscan.

12. **Protect the screen.** Nothing stays pixel-still for hours (OLED burn-in): the sky lives, the sheet drifts imperceptibly, the piece rotates, the night page rearranges everything.

13. **American English, AP style.** "7:30 a.m.", "noon", "Nov. 16", "Thu.". Copy is short, warm, and plain, like a kind neighbor, never like software ("The TV didn't answer. Try again.", not "Error 502").

## III. Engineering

14. **Simple, boring, few.** One Next.js app on Vercel. One small store (Upstash Redis). No database, no queue, no second server until something truly needs one. Every dependency, service, and env var is a cost that has to be justified in the change that adds it. Deleting code is a feature.

15. **Fail gently.** Every source can be missing or down (weather, calendar, notebook, store, voice, nature clips) and the page still appears. A clip is loaded before its turn; one that isn't ready, won't load, or stalls (a drive pulled out) is skipped, and the turn goes to the next thing. Never a black frame. Where there are no clips, nature is home. A missing note is left out, never replaced by an error, a stack trace, or a spinner on the TV. Offline, the TV keeps showing what it has.

16. **The TV is the brain.** The TV decides what's on screen. Everything else (the phone remote, Siri, Alexa, the voice) only _asks_, by posting to `/api/show`. Requests go stale after two minutes, so a TV that wakes up never obeys yesterday's.

17. **Public repo, private family.** The code is public; the family is not. Never commit secrets, the family's location, its teams (they say where it lives: FAMILY_TEAMS, in env), calendar contents, names, photos, or the notebook corpus (James Clear's writing is copyrighted; it lives in the store, never in git), or the nature clips (for the family's own use: they live on the TV, served by its shell, never in git and never online; the code may only say "nature clips"). Every route that shows family data checks `isFamily()`. Production with no `FAMILY_KEY` is locked, not open.

18. **Time is the family's time.** Every date shown or reasoned about is in `HOME_TIME_ZONE`, through `src/lib/time.ts`. Servers run in UTC; never let that leak onto the page.

19. **Look at the screen.** No design change is done until you've seen it at 1920 × 1080 (`node scripts/shot.mjs`), by day and at night, and watched the ink move. Tests prove the logic; only eyes prove the magic.

## IV. Voice

20. **The voice speaks briefly, then leaves.** One or two sentences. It answers, shows the right page, says goodbye, and hangs up on its own. It never asks a follow-up question to keep someone talking, never suggests more screen time, never sells. Children may be the ones talking.

21. **The voice tells the truth.** It answers from the snapshot (the weather, the calendar), not from imagination. If it doesn't know, it says so in a few words.

## Vocabulary

| Word             | Means                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The app**      | The Stewart Family App: the screen, the voice, the remote. Its name is one constant in `src/config.ts`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Page**         | A full-screen view: `home`, `weather`, `week`, `notebook` (shown as "Ideas"), `sports` (the games), `nature`, and the automatic night page.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Hero**         | The one piece set in the middle of home.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Corner notes** | The hour, the sky, and what's next, written small at home's edges.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Snapshot**     | The data the TV draws from (weather, happenings, notebook), refreshed every few minutes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Mood**         | Time of day, set by the sun: morning, afternoon, evening (5 p.m. until the first stars, the paper dimming after sunset), night (from the first stars, halfway through dusk, to halfway through dawn). Night darkens the paper. The question for the table is asked only from 2 to 6 p.m., by the clock, whatever the mood. |
| **Nature clip**  | A short film of a real place with natural sound only, kept on the TV; home shows a minute of one now and then, and the nature page plays them one after another.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Ink**          | The transition system. Content is "inked in" and "sinks out".                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Show request** | "Put this page on the TV", from `/remote`, Siri, Alexa, or the voice, via `POST /api/show`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Notebook**     | Ideas, quotes, and questions (James Clear's 3-2-1, or the small commonplace book in the repo).                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Game**         | One line on the sports page: a game of the family's teams or the Top 25, with its hour, channel, and chance of winning (or its score).                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Happening**    | One calendar event occurrence.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Family key**   | The one secret. A few plain words, easy to type with a remote.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
